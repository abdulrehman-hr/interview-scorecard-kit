# Interviewer Guide

How to run a structured interview with this kit — before, during, and after.

## Before the interview (15 min prep)

1. **Re-read the CV and the job description** side by side. Note 2-3 claims to probe.
2. **Pick 6-8 questions** from `question-bank.md` — same core set for every candidate
   applying to this role. Add 1-2 role-specific probes.
3. **Set the scorecard weights** for the role (defaults in the template are a
   starting point). Keep weights identical across all candidates for the role.
4. **Agree on who asks what** if panel interviewing — no repeat questions, no
   "good cop / bad cop" theater.

## During the interview (30-45 min)

Follow the **5-25-5 structure**:

- **First 5 min:** Warm-up (Section A questions). Put them at ease. Sell the role briefly.
- **Middle 25 min:** Behavioral + situational questions (Sections B-G). Dig with
  follow-ups: *"What exactly did YOU do?"*, *"What happened as a result?"*,
  *"What would you do differently?"*
- **Last 5 min:** Their questions (Section H). Never skip this — their questions
  reveal as much as their answers.

**Ground rules:**

- Take notes on **facts and quotes**, not impressions. Write "described cutting
  onboarding time from 10 to 4 days" — not "impressive."
- Don't score during the interview. It splits your attention and anchors you early.
- It's fine to say "let me think about that" — silence is a tool, not a failure.
- End on time. Respecting the candidate's time is employer branding.

## After the interview (10 min — do it NOW)

1. **Score immediately**, while memory is fresh. Don't check with other interviewers first.
2. Score each criterion **1-5** on evidence, not gut feel. If you can't point to
   something they said, you can't give a 5.
3. Write the **single strongest reason to hire** and the **single biggest concern**.
   Forcing one of each fights halo/horns bias.
4. Only then compare scorecards with fellow interviewers.

## Bias traps (and how to dodge them)

| Trap | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Halo effect | Great first 2 minutes → you inflate everything after | Score each criterion separately, on evidence |
| Similarity bias | "They're like me, they'll be great" | Ask: would I score this answer the same from anyone? |
| Confirmation bias | You decided in minute 3 and spent 30 min confirming it | Write your score BEFORE discussing with others |
| Contrast effect | Average candidate looks amazing right after a terrible one | Score against the rubric, not against the last person |
| Gut feel | "I just have a good feeling" | Feelings are data about you, not about them — find the evidence |

## The hiring decision

- Average weighted score **4.0+**: Strong hire — move fast.
- **3.0-3.9**: Hire if references check out; discuss concerns explicitly.
- **Below 3.0**: Pass. A "maybe" hire is usually a "no" you're postponing.
- **Any single 1** on integrity, honesty, or values: automatic no, regardless of total.

Never let one interviewer's veto be silent — but never let one interviewer's
enthusiasm override two colleagues' documented concerns either. The scorecards
are the discussion, not a decoration for a decision already made.
